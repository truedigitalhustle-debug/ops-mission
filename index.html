The method

Build it in 10-second pieces.

A minute-plus video is not one generation. It's a stack of short clips that share the same references, stitched together. Here's the whole workflow.

Make your reference images first.One full-body photo of you on a plain background. Then generate the rest with the image prompts below: a squadmate, the compound, the basement, and the thing in the basement. Every clip pulls from this same set, so they all match.
Lock your character in full gear.Generate one clip of yourself kitted up, then screenshot the clearest frame and use THAT as your reference from then on. If you only reference your plain photo, the AI invents a new helmet and goggles every clip. This is the fix.
Generate each scene as its own 10-second clip.Seedance 2.5, 9:16, 720p, 10 seconds. Load the references in the order the prompt expects, so @image1 is you, @image2 is the next one, and so on. Paste the prompt. Generate.
Check every clip before you move on.Look at the goggles, the weapon, which way the barrel points. If something's off, regenerate that one clip. Never fix it in the next one.
Stitch in CapCut.Drop the clips in order, trim the dead air, add a hook line at the top and your CTA at the end.

What I learned the hard way

Five things that cost me wasted generations

Lock the gear, not just the face. My goggles changed from two-tube to four-tube and back across clips until I referenced a frame of myself in full kit instead of my plain photo. Whatever is in the reference is what stays consistent.
Keep dangerous things outside the frame they'd break. "A rocket streaks past the door" put the rocket through the cabin. "40 meters from the helicopter, never entering the cabin, only its light comes in" kept it outside.
Put the target and the barrel in the same shot. My first rooftop shot had my guy aiming the wrong way. A camera behind his shoulder, looking at the building, fixed it in one try.
If you're shooting at something, it has to be there. Write "in frame in every shot and the target of every shot fired." Otherwise the AI will happily fire at a wall.
Re-tag the reference on every mention. Not just "the operator," but "operator @image1 " every single time, with a plain space on both sides of the tag. That's how the model knows who you mean.

Save your 10 seconds

When a generation is half right, don't throw it out. Keep the good shots, regenerate only the broken beat as a short clip, and cut them together. Half of this video is spliced that way.
